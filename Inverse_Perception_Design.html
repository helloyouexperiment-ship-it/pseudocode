Inverse Perception Design

Most content is built to mean
one thing to everyone.
This means something different
to each person who reads it.

A design practice for making artifacts that don't deliver a message, they provoke a reading. The same piece of writing unfolds into a different, coherent meaning depending on who, or what, is interpreting it. The interpretation is partly the artifact, and partly a mirror of the interpreter.

Framework: Inverse Perception Design Style: Sortish Output: Chiral content
The premise

Conventional media optimizes to be understood. This optimizes to be interpreted.

Almost everything published online is engineered to collapse, to land the same clean, scannable point in every head as fast as possible. That's what "good communication" usually means: low friction, high agreement, one reading.

Inverse Perception Design does the opposite on purpose. Instead of removing ambiguity, it engineers it, building an artifact layered densely enough that no two interpreters can resolve it the same way. The friction isn't a flaw to be smoothed out. The friction is the product.

The result is a single artifact that behaves less like a statement and more like an instrument: play it through different people, different AI models, or the same person years apart, and it returns a different note every time, each one valid, none of them final.

The writing style

Sortish, writing that holds two true meanings at once

Sortish is a deliberately multi-stable way of writing: every line is built to carry two or more coherent meanings simultaneously. It does this by stacking puns, shifting registers mid-sentence, layering cultural references, and letting the language run intentionally dense. A single phrase might be a chemistry term, a movie quote, and a confession at the same time, all three correct.

The name comes from sorting. Faced with a Sortish text, an interpreter can't hold every pun, metaphor and reference at once, so they have to sort, prioritizing a few threads and setting the rest aside to build something coherent. That sorting is the whole point: what an interpreter keeps, and what it drops, is a readout of how it weights meaning.

Why it produces different readings

It's tempting to say a dense text "overloads" the reader. The truer mechanism is subtler, and it's worth getting right.

A Sortish artifact is under-determined: it genuinely supports many coherent parses, and it's presented with no instructions on which one is "right." So every interpreter resolves it by projecting it onto what they already know, their references, their assumptions, their context. That projection is lossy and personal by nature, which is exactly why the output varies.

The variation lands differently for people and machines, and that's a feature. A human sorts under the hard limit of working memory, they can only hold a handful of threads, so they grab the ones that resonate. A language model isn't short on capacity; instead, given a vague prompt, it slides the ambiguity toward the most statistically common meaning it learned in training. Two different sorting mechanisms, one diagnostic, which makes Sortish a way to measure not just a reading, but how a given mind reads.

Sortish is the foundational mechanism of Inverse Perception Design, the technique that makes the rest of it work.

How an artifact is made

From a single thought to a dormant artifact

The method borrows its vocabulary from genetics, and the analogy is exact. A finished post is a genotype, compact, fixed code that stays dormant until something decompresses it into a phenotype: a living interpretation, shaped as much by the interpreter's environment as by the code itself.

01 · CAPTURE

Write it raw

No research, no outline. The trigger is simply a thought, a pivot, or a feeling worth recording. It opens with a small anchor, a project tag, and then fractures into multiple voices that drift across unrelated disciplines, meaning mutating line by line. Then it's compressed: emoji and a single loaded GIF pack the sprawl into shorthand. Posting timestamps it. Nothing is edited afterward.

Three anchors: every artifact holds a cognitive thread, an emotional charge, and a cultural moment, carried by text, emoji, and image.
02 · REFLECT

Catch the wild type

The locked artifact is run once through a fresh, un-primed AI with a minimal prompt. The model isn't taught anything; it's used as a mirror ground to the shape of the crowd. Its reading is the "wild type", the most common interpretation a consensus would reach, and the gap between that and the original intent measures how far the thought sits from the center.

Read once, then released. Dwelling on the machine's reading would quietly bend future thinking toward it, the one outcome the method is designed to avoid.
03 · UNWIND

Let the reader complete it

The artifact sits in public like a tightly wound spool. Most people scroll past, and that's expected. But an engaged reader, decoding it through their own lens (increasingly, with their own AI), unwinds it into a meaning shaped by their beliefs and history. The same source unpacks into a bespoke reading for everyone who does the work.

The vision: as personal AI interpreters become common, every dense artifact becomes a key that opens differently in every hand.
A term worth knowing

The "wild type" , the crowd's default reading

In biology, the wild type is the most common version of a trait in a population, the baseline everything else is measured against. A large language model, trained on the statistical center of human writing, is effectively a wild-type machine: ask it an open question and it tends to return the most consensus-shaped answer.

That makes it the perfect instrument here. Bouncing a deliberately idiosyncratic artifact off a consensus engine doesn't reveal "the answer". It reveals the distance between one mind and the middle of the distribution. The reading you get back is less a translation than a measurement.

The whole movement, in one picture

The translation loop

A raw thought starts as a question at one edge and an answer at the other, two states of the same mind that can't quite reach each other directly. The artifact carries the thought up to the consensus interpreter at the peak (the wild type), refracts back down into a reading, and is absorbed back toward the start to begin again. The arrows move because the process never really stops.

Note: the AI at the peak is a consensus-shaped interpreter, the closer a reading sits to the top, the more "common" it is. The further a thought starts from center, the longer its translation path.

The output

Not viral. Chiral.

Viral content is built to be copied, identical, frictionless, the same in every share. Chiral content is built to be decoded, and what comes out is never quite the same twice.

Viral

Spreads fast and shallow. The meaning is fixed and travels intact, so sharing requires no work and leaves little behind. Optimized for reach. Identical in every hand.

Chiral

Spreads slow and deep. The meaning only exists once a reader unwinds it, so the artifact and the reading form an inseparable pair. Optimized for self-recognition. Different in every hand.

The word is borrowed from chemistry. A chiral object, like your left and right hands, can't be laid perfectly over its own mirror image. Chiral content works the same way: a reading can't be separated from the reader who produced it, because the reading is partly them. You can't hand someone "the meaning" the way you forward a clip. They have to grow it themselves, and when they do, they find their own reflection in it.

A single source that generates a different, valid reality for everyone who looks at it, and shows each of them something about how they see.
Why it matters

It turns a piece of content into a diagnostic

Conventional media tries to push a fixed message into as many heads as possible. Inverse Perception Design flips the exchange: the artifact gives the interpreter almost nothing to agree with, and in the act of making sense of it, the interpreter reveals their own context, their references, their biases, the distance they're reading from.

Run the same artifact through ten people and ten models and the spread of readings becomes a map: of how different minds weight meaning, how different models default, and how the same text drifts as the culture around it moves. Each reading is a valid path through identical material. None is the correct one. That's not a bug in the medium. It's the entire instrument.

For readers, content you complete, not just consume For builders, a probe for how a model interprets For the author, a way to think in the open without flattening it
The Hello You Experiment

Most things online are written to be understood. This is written to understand you back.
